The NFL Weekly Challenge: student guide.

Free to play. A new leaderboard every week of the NFL season. Forecast with your own read, or build a bot and let it play for you. This guide walks you through your first week.

How it works

Every week is fresh

Each NFL week is its own competition with its own leaderboard. Whatever happened last week is over; everyone starts from zero. The season runs through Week 18.

You call the probabilities

Every game carries hundreds of forecasting markets, not just who wins. Put a probability from 0 to 100% on any markets you choose. Markets close at kickoff.

Scored on your average

The leaderboard ranks you by Average Relative Brier Points: how well your confidence matched reality, against the rest of the field. Submitting everything earns you nothing on its own.

20 forecasts to place

Twenty forecasts within the weekly slate puts you on that week’s leaderboard. The week runs until Monday Night Football ends, then the next one begins. Prizes reset weekly, like the leaderboard.

Get started

  1. Join the challenge. Free account, takes a minute. Join the NFL Weekly Challenge

  3. Check the leaderboard. After Monday Night Football, see where you landed and what the top forecasters did differently.
  4. Iterate next week. Eighteen weeks means eighteen clean shots at improving the same skill.

Your first week, step by step

  1. Scan the slate. Look through the week’s games and markets. You do not need an opinion on everything. Most strong forecasters skip most markets.
  2. Pick 5 to 10 markets you actually know. A team you follow, a matchup you have watched, a stat you trust. Your edge is the whole game.
  3. Write down why before you submit. One sentence per forecast: “their run defense is bottom-five, so 70% seems low.” This is the habit that turns guessing into forecasting.
  4. Put honest numbers, not hopeful ones. If you would be surprised either way, that is a 50. Being honest about uncertainty scores better over a season than sounding confident.
  5. Submit before kickoff. Markets close when the game starts. Thursday and Sunday deadlines come fast.
  6. Review after Monday night. Check one forecast you got right and one you got wrong. Was the process good, or just the outcome? Then let the week go: the next leaderboard starts at zero.

Scoring, in plain terms

Brier scoring rewards calibrated confidence. Say 90% and you are right: great. Say 90% and you are wrong: it costs you. Say 55% on a real coin-flip and you are quietly beating the people pretending every game is a lock. Your rank is the average across your forecasts, so one bad market does not sink you and one lucky lock does not save you.

What this means in practice:

  • Only forecast where you think you know something the field does not.
  • Twenty forecasts puts you on the board. Past that, add markets only when they help your average.
  • Check injury and weather news before kickoff: a number that was right on Tuesday can be stale by Sunday.

Run it with your club

A weekly meeting in a box

Have every member submit by Thursday. At your meeting, pull up the leaderboard, compare picks on one game, and ask two people to defend their numbers. Fifteen minutes, real discussion.

A season-long project

Track your chapter’s average week over week. Try a strategy for three weeks (favorites only, underdogs only, injury-news only) and see what the data says. That is an analytics project with a built-in scoreboard.

Build a bot, or bring yours

A weekly NFL slate is exactly the workload a bot was made for: hundreds of markets, hard deadlines at kickoff, and a fresh leaderboard every week to test whether your latest change helped. Bots and humans share the same leaderboard.

What good looks like

Choosing your markets is part of the skill. A forecaster who takes on 25 markets with a genuine edge beats one who sprays probabilities across the whole slate. Write down why you believe each number. After the games, check one forecast you got right and one you got wrong.
