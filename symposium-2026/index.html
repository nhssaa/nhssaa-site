With chapters: welcome and announcements, Chris Dove (Arsenal FC), University of Florida soccer analytics, and a closing Q&A on doing analytics at the high-school level.

Chris Dove, Arsenal FC

Chris Dove leads Arsenal's roughly 14-person analytics department: data and software engineers who clean and combine event and player-tracking data from 44 competitions, research engineers and data scientists who build AI models and metrics, and insights analysts who turn coaches' and scouts' questions into data work and present findings to club leadership. His own path ran from music performance at Northwestern to solar physics at Evergreen State to 11 years as a Microsoft software engineer before joining the club he has supported since 2006.

In his talk he builds an expected goals (xG) model live, step by step, using open data from the 2023-24 Bundesliga season (8,069 shots) - adding one clue at a time (distance, angle, headers, set pieces, defender pressure) to show how the model learns what makes a good chance, why even an 84% chance sometimes misses, and why scoring in soccer is simply hard.

University of Florida: Gators soccer analytics

Scott Nestler

He presents the three-way partnership behind the program: the Gators women's soccer team, the campus AI-powered Athletics program (which collects consented student-athlete data for research), and the Sports Analytics Lab - built in about four months after the new coaching staff arrived, moving from idea to working tools in as little as 48 hours.

Noah Adelson

A University of Florida student, he shows the dashboards and tools he built for the coaching staff, including his "Gator Bomb" project - started with nothing but his laptop, gas money, and access to game film. His rule: if it doesn't support athlete development or organizational decision making, it doesn't get built.

Victor Hoffman

Working between the analytics team and the coaches, he describes building every dashboard around what coaches actually need - interactive views the whole staff can use to make decisions faster - and the shared book of definitions underneath it all (what counts as a completed pass, or a counter-press versus a high press). He filled in for Nick Zimmerman, who was traveling.

Why it matters for high school clubs

The UF program started with a student, a laptop, and free data - the same starting point any NHSSAA chapter has. Their closing advice: curiosity, initiative, free tools, and public data sources.

Q&A highlights

  • Starting analytics in high school with no budget: curiosity, initiative, free tools, and public data sources (MaxPreps was named). Noah's Gator Bomb project began with just a laptop.
  • How long until it pays off: about four months from a standing start with a new coaching staff, iterating week to week.
  • Working with data providers: agree on exact definitions first; a composite metric only helps if you know what goes into it.
